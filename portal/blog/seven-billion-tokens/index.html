Seven Billion Tokens, Dissected.

One business intent, seven months, and where the speed actually came from — a complete anatomy of a real case, with provenance on every number.

7,007,437,567 recorded tokens 52 machine-readable constraints 17.1 → 0.3 human turns per commit evidence before claims

By BG1SB  ·  Sep 5, 2026  ·  updated Sep 8, 2026  ·  ~30 min read

For anyone who wants to do business with AI. This is the full dissection of one real case: the same operator, the same amateur-radio ecosystem, seven months (2026-03 → 2026-09, plus a 2025-10 → 2026-04 control period), 7.1B recorded tokens, seven repositories, six agent harnesses, a dozen models. It answers one through-line: how a business intent becomes a delivered product; how many round-trips the process took and why it kept getting smoother; what models, tools and method each contributed; and how you might spend your next seven months. Every number has a source, fact and inference are labelled separately, and the measurement boundaries live in the appendix.

TL;DR — the skeleton in five findings

  • FactThe efficiency curve is real. Three repos, one direction: human intervention per commit fell from 17.1 to 0.3 turns on ft8 (~30×). "Getting smoother" has a quantified shape.
  • FactTokens track uncertainty, not output. ~26,800 tokens per line on ft8's DSP and timing-safety code versus 136 on a read-only reference tree — a ~200× density gap. The meter is an uncertainty gauge, not a productivity trophy.
  • InferenceMethod is the variable you control. The same model cost 75.5 M tokens per commit exploring ft8 and 0.9–3.8 M in maintenance. Models set the ceiling; method decides how much of it you actually collect.
  • FactThe hardest evidence is incident-to-immunity time. 13 days (frequency drift) and 7 days (backup disk) from field incident to a gate that makes the same mistake impossible.
  • ThesisOne-sentence discipline: first ask "where does my judgment settle", then ask "what can AI do for me".

Volume I · Business & Requirements

Intent never starts with "AI"

Every product in this ecosystem began as a sentence in business language — and the sentence still stands with the word "AI" removed.

01 · Intent

Four business intents, none of them mentions AI

AI was brought in later as a means of execution. It was never the purpose.

MRRC · 2026-03-06 · ba66892

Hams needed a universal remote station interface — drive any Hamlib-capable radio from a browser, without rewriting the control logic for each radio. Save effort.

SunMRRC / SunsdrMobile · 2026-06-21 · 38cb85e

SunSDR2 DX users needed to bypass the desktop software and get synchronized control, spectrum, audio, and deterministic transmit safety. Safety.

MRRC FT-710 · 2026-07-06 · 9403e2e

The FT-710 already had a USB cable plugged into it — use that cable instead of the SCU-LAN10 accessory for remote operation. (Proven later: the in-cable FT4222 bridge yields ~21 fps of real spectrum, 48 kHz bidirectional Opus audio, and full CAT control.) Save money.

MRRC-FT8 · 2026-08-03

FT8 is not a single CAT command — it is a complete workflow of 15-second cycle synchronization, decode selection, a QSO state machine, and controlled transmit timing. Save time.

Author's thesis

First rule: if removing the word "AI" kills your business need, it was never a business need — it was a technology demo.

02 · Control group

Six months with nowhere for knowledge to land

The models were not weak. What was weak: knowledge had no carrier.

From 2025-10-20 to 2026-04-16, the same person ran 185 iFlow sessions and 37,202 turns across six model backends (qwen3-coder-plus, glm-5 — 10,420 turns in a single week — kimi-k2.5, minimax-m2.5). The output was real: MRRC's ancestor was born in this period. But nothing about the work settled anywhere.

Fact

Every one of the 37,202 assistant turns records zero usage, and no engineering artifact survived the era: no contract, no document system, no gate. When a session ended, what it had learned ended with it.

That is the problem every mechanism in Volume III exists to solve.

Volume takeaway

Intent exists in business language or it does not exist; and knowledge needs a carrier that outlives the conversation. Every mechanism in the volumes that follow exists for the second half.

05 · Rework

Not a monotonic decline — an arc

Rework concentrates in exploration and integration weeks; maintenance weeks sit in single digits.

Fact

ft710's fix-commit share fell from 36% in its opening week to 6–12% in maintenance weeks; ft8's fell from 29% to 17% — achieved during its two highest-volume weeks (147 / 82 commits).

The counter-evidence is just as real. MRRC hit 50% fix share in its big integration week (W32, 798M tokens); modern hit 31% in its big refactor week (W35); sunsdr's early hardware-tuning weeks hit 51%. New requirements always bring new rework. The difference is who does the rework: increasingly, agents complete it autonomously (modern W35: fix share 31%, but humans intervened only 0.71 turns per commit).

06 · Cadence

The workflow takes shape

Contract red first, agent delivers to green.

Fact

Releases accelerated: modern shipped 6 versions in 13 days (3 in a single day); ft8 shipped 3 versions in its first 3 days; MRRC once shipped 9 versions in two days.

Fact

Since 2026-W35 the website repo shows a stable "contract test goes red first, feature commit turns it green" pattern: 5 test→feat pairs, with a median time-to-green of 19 minutes (example: on 09-05 the contract test went red first, and the article feature went green 19 minutes later).

Inference

Write the contract first and let AI deliver to green — an institutionalized capability that only formed late, and the organizational shape of "smooth".

Volume takeaway

Three curves fall in the same direction; rework follows an arc and concentrates in exploration weeks. The late-timeline difference is not zero rework — it is who does it: the agent, autonomously.

Volume III · Method

What the harness and the SDD did at each stage

The method's growth is precisely datable in git history, and its hardest evidence is a chain that starts with a field incident.

The division of labour: the outer harness defines business, technical and product constraints — why the work exists, where the boundaries are, what evidence can change a conclusion. The inner loop — Specify → Implement → Test → Review → Observe → Update SDD — is delegated to agents wholesale. The living SDD lets design intent flow downward and field evidence flow back up, with explicit lifecycle states and no automatic promotion.

08 · Incident chains

The hardest evidence that the method works

Not one of the 52 constraints was designed cleverly in advance; every one traces back to a concrete incident.

FT-710 frequency drift · 13-day loop

DN; is not a DNR query but a VFO step command — polling it every 2 seconds drifted a live radio's frequency by ~20 Hz per call. Field incident (fixed 07-07, 72fd6f0) → design ruling AD-014 written into the SDD (07-08, bb128ff) → machine-readable constraint cat-no-dn (07-20, 2498ec2) → pre-edit interception, reproducible (exit code 2).

Deploy backups fill the disk · 7-day loop

08-29: server /var/tmp at 100% (~300 MB per backup) → 09-05: sister fixes land in four repositories on the same day — and, following the thread, the hidden defect that portal backups had never worked (an unquoted remote heredoc, 067f565) → the rules are promoted to the four deployment iron rules.

Author's thesis

The incident conversion rate — the time from incident to permanent immunity — is the true measure of an organization's learning ability.

09 · Delivery evidence

Two ledgers that never vouch for each other

Test suites were run, not read, on census day 2026-09-05.

Fact

FT-710: Ran 439 tests / OK. modern: Ran 682 tests — the 1 error is a Windows-only launcher failing to import on macOS, an environment limit, not a product defect. ft8: 937 tests collected — collected is not passed, and the wording must keep the distinction. MRRC V5.6.5 was field-verified and released (it entered V6.0.0 on 09-05). sunsdr V1.0 shipped with PTT two-way ACK.

Ledger A · Product maturity

  1. design target
  2. simulation
  3. automated test
  4. bench verified
  5. field verified
  6. released / operational
  7. known issue (the honest rung)

Ledger B · Process evidence

  1. registry entry exists
  2. spec / plan trail
  3. enforced inside sessions
  4. interception reproduced

The two ledgers never intersect: a pretty process ledger never vouches for a product claim. The open boundaries stay open in public: FT710Mobile has an unresolved P0 PTT safety issue, and the EFHW tuner's claim status stays at design-target until PCB fabrication and bench verification.

Author's thesis

You can delegate the doing; you can never delegate the "does it count". Responsibility cannot be delegated.

Volume takeaway

The method was not designed in advance; it grew out of incidents. Verdict → machine-readable constraint → pre-write interception is the loop — and all 52 constraints trace back to a specific decision or accident.

Volume V · Attribution & Advice

Where the efficiency actually came from

Two variables moved at once for seven months. Here is the evidence that separates them.

13 · Attribution

Stronger models, or a thicker method?

Both variables changed over the seven months; four pieces of evidence separate them.

1 · Same model, ±method: an order of magnitude

Flash exploring ft8 in W32 cost 75.5 M per commit; the same model in maintenance cost 0.9–3.8 M per commit. The model did not change — the contract thickness did.

2 · Strong models, no method: still zero

iFlow-era glm-5 produced 10,420 turns in a single week, yet the 37,202 turns left zero sediment. With method absent, model strength is beside the point.

3 · The method's birthday is precisely visible

The contract package grew in ft710 on 07-20 and was inherited wholesale by ft8 on 08-02 — and ft8's intervention turns fell from 17.1 straight to 0.3.

4 · Model upgrades widen the delegation radius

The task granularity one turn can reliably deliver grew from "change this function" to "deliver this feature against this spec and pass the contract tests". Models decide what can be delegated; method decides whether what was delegated comes back.

Conclusion · Inference

The attribution ratio cannot be separated precisely from this data, but the direction is clear: model capability sets the ceiling, and engineering method decides how much of that ceiling you actually capture. Of the two variables, only one is under your control.

14 · Advice

Two tracks

The engineering track is yours; the model track is the market's. The compound interest is on yours.

The engineering-capability track (you control it — the compound interest is here):

  1. Acceptance before implementation

    Write the acceptance conditions before the first line of code — the standard of "does it count" precedes implementation and is the number-one killer of round-trips.

  2. Machine-readable constraints, pre-write gates

    The fix is only the first link; ruling → machine-readable constraint → gate is the closed loop (this ecosystem: 13 days / 7 days).

  3. Red first, then green

    Contract tests first, agents deliver to green — the median loop in this ecosystem is 19 minutes.

  4. Tokens as an uncertainty meter

    Wherever the token peak lands, that is where the direction is undecided.

The model-capability track (the market gives it — the strategy is not locking in):

  1. Pick tiers by task, not by faith

    Flagships for greenfield and blitz work, the value tier for daily work, long-context variants for specials — in measurement, no model wins every task.

  2. Re-evaluate the market every 3–4 weeks

    The timeline above changed main force five times; the selection mechanism — fast trials plus a weekly review — matters more than any single choice.

  3. Beware model names

    Routing labels are not model entities, and vendor claims are not independent benchmarks. Your own logs — which task, how many tokens, how many round-trips, what result — are the only benchmark that never lies to you.

15 · Closing

Ask where it settles first

Seven months of lessons fold into one sentence.

Author's thesis

First ask "where does my judgment settle", then ask "what can AI do for me".

AI use without a settling ground is consumption — 37,202 turns of conversation can leave nothing behind. AI use with a settling ground is asset accumulation — every incident, every constraint, every spec lowers the cost of the next delegation. Models change generation every quarter; the contract thickens every day. The former is weather; the latter is climate. What you can build is the climate.

Volume takeaway

Models set the ceiling; method decides how much of it you collect. Of the two variables, only one is yours to build — the sentence above says where to start.

Volume VI · The Four Dimensions

Separate judgment from execution

The distillation layer. Four dimensions explain everything the ledger showed — and they travel to any business, radio or not.

16 · The four dimensions

Four dimensions, four disciplines

The merged distillation: what the case means once the ledger is closed.

The one-sentence method

Agentic engineering is not "using AI to write code". It separates judgment from execution: judgment settles into contracts, execution is delegated to agents, and evidence decides whether a contract survives. That one sentence explains why the same operator left evaporated chat logs in the control period and seven repositories with 52 machine-readable constraints in the measured one — the difference was never the model; it was whether judgment had a carrier that outlives the conversation.

1 · Engineering history — the abstraction ladder rises again

Machine code → assembly → high-level languages → frameworks → cloud: every generation delegates one more layer of "how" to the machine. Agentic delegates the whole implementation layer. What remains for the person is three acts: define the intent, mark the boundaries, exercise the verdict. The workload moved accordingly — 12 design specs and 10 implementation plans were written before the code; ft8's first substantive commit brought AGENTS.md, a 15-chapter SDD and contract tests before any business code. Definition now precedes implementation.

2 · Epistemology — the carrier decides the knowledge's lifespan

Chat logs evaporate with the session; documents expire and get skipped; machine-readable constraints with runtime gates do not need to be remembered — they appear when needed. 52 constraints are 52 judgments made permanent, and they survived six harnesses because the carrier is the repository, not a tool. Incident-to-immunity time is the true measure of organizational learning: this ecosystem ran 13 days and 7 days.

3 · Economics — tokens are the price of uncertainty

Token spend follows uncertainty density, not line count: ~26,800 tokens per line on ft8's DSP and timing-safety code versus 136 on a read-only reference tree. Peaks land exactly where direction is undecided. And 95.2% of recorded tokens are cache-reads — the tax of contracts that are not yet machine-sliceable. Spending less is not "use AI less"; it is reducing the uncertainty itself.

4 · Responsibility — two ledgers, three non-delegable acts

Product maturity and process evidence are separate books that never vouch for each other: on census day, 439 passing FT-710 tests could not close one open P0 PTT safety issue in the client. Three acts are never delegable — hardware bring-up and bench debugging, RF safety on real radios, and the verdict on whether evidence is sufficient. Not because AI is too weak, but because responsibility cannot be delegated.

The four disciplines

1 · Definition before implementation — acceptance criteria, non-goals and boundaries written before the first line of code; intent phrased in business language that stands without the word AI. 2 · Every incident becomes a rule — repair is only the first link; verdict → constraint → pre-write interception closes the loop. 3 · Watch where uncertainty spends — token peaks are a direction radar; audit every important number against two sources. 4 · Keep the two ledgers apart — process badges never promote product claims; "do" can be delegated, "did it count" never leaves your hands.

Appendix C · Measurement boundaries

Eight honesty statements

Every number in this article lives inside these boundaries.

  1. Tokens cannot be attributed to single commits; every "commit × token" correspondence here is an aggregation at working-directory × time-window granularity.
  2. The claude-code dual caliber is documented in Appendix A; codex turns are counted as token_count events.
  3. Retention bias: claude-code logs are retained only back to 2026-08-05; iFlow and Hermes record no tokens; sessions started from a parent directory cannot be attributed to a project. All totals are lower bounds.
  4. Turns/commit counts only human-input turns; MRRC W25's 27.6 turns/commit is affected by codex's archive caliber and is treated as doubtful.
  5. Task-type classification is keyword-based on the first message of each session, with n<65 per model — inference-grade, read for structure only.
  6. Sessions are attributed to the model with the highest token share; mixed sessions hide secondary models' contributions.
  7. ft710 and modern share 149 commits; commit classification by message prefix is coarse, with about ±10% error; weeks with <5 commits are excluded from trend judgments.
  8. Public benchmark numbers come from external sources (AA / LMArena / Code Arena / vendor announcements), with vendor claims and independent measurements labelled in separate layers; big-pickle, code-supernova-1-million and kimi-for-coding are routing labels with no official entity behind them.
