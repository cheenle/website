Seven Billion Tokens, Dissected.

One business intent, seven months, eleven agent harnesses — and a published number that turned out to be wrong in three directions at once.

7,404,583,808 recorded tokens +397,146,241 vs the published census 52 machine-readable constraints evidence before claims

By BG1SB  ·  Sep 5, 2026  ·  updated Sep 12, 2026  ·  ~18 min read

IntentProcessMethodModelVerdictDimensions

For anyone who wants to do business with AI. This is the dissection of one real case: the same operator, the same amateur-radio ecosystem, seven months (2026-03 → 2026-09, plus a 2025-10 → 2026-04 control period), seven repositories, eleven agent harnesses that record usage, and a dozen models. It answers one through-line: how a business intent becomes a delivered product, how many round-trips the process took and why it kept getting smoother, and what the numbers behind that story can actually support. Every figure carries a source and a cutoff; fact and inference are labelled separately; the ledger tables and the measurement boundaries live in the companion volumes so this page can stay a narrative.

TL;DR — the skeleton in five findings

  • FactA ledger can be wrong in both directions at once. The published census of 7,007,437,567 tokens was overstated (one harness double-counted chunks of the same API response, ~2.7×) and understated (a harness marked "not recorded" had 485,213,348 tokens sitting in a table nobody queried). The honest re-run is 7,404,583,808.
  • FactA ledger is a reading, not an asset. One harness deleted its own history mid-week (down 679,899,086 tokens); a re-run of the same script twelve minutes later differed by 51,427,037 because the session doing the measuring was itself being measured.
  • FactThe efficiency curve is real. Three repos, one direction: human intervention per commit fell from 17.1 to 0.3 turns on ft8 (~30×), and last week mrrc_modern ran at 0.73 turns per commit across 41 commits.
  • InferenceModel capability sets the ceiling; method decides how much of it you collect. The same model cost 75.5 M tokens per commit exploring ft8 and 0.9 M in maintenance. Of the two variables, only one is under your control.
  • ThesisOne-sentence discipline: first ask "where does my judgment settle", then ask "what can AI do for me" — and snapshot the evidence before you quote it.

01 · Prologue

The ledger that shrank

Before any argument about method, one lesson about measurement — learned the hard way.

On 2026-09-05 this site published a token census: 7,007,437,567 recorded tokens. One week later the same measurement was re-run and returned 7,404,583,808 — 397,146,241 more. The interesting part is not the growth. It is that the old number was wrong in three separate ways at the same time.

DirectionWhat happenedEvidence
Overstatedclaude-code's log writes one usage record per chunk of the same API response, so summing every line inflates the total by roughly 2.7×. Deduplicating by message.id leaves 818,520,401 — and brings the whole ledger to 5,695,506,675.6,600 distinct API responses
UnderstatedHermes was published as "not recorded". It records fine — in a sessions table holding 485,213,348 tokens. The earlier check queried messages.token_count, which is all zeros.292 sessions, 2026-05-15 → 07-24
Driftingclaude-code deleted its own older logs at 2026-09-07T13:13:57Z, so the same harness measured 679,899,086 tokens less than a week before. Then a second run of the identical script, twelve minutes later, differed by 51,427,037 — because the session performing the measurement was itself writing to the logs being measured.Observer effect, reproduced twice

The counter-check matters as much as the finding. codex has been dormant since late August, and its figure came back bit-for-bit identical at 547,796,667 — measured twice, by two different code paths. A stable measurement and an unstable one are both information; only the stable one deserves to be called a total.

Fact

All figures on this page are a snapshot taken at 2026-09-12T02:40:59Z across eleven harnesses. The full per-harness table, each harness's own measurement window, and the rules for what may and may not be compared are in the companion volume The Ledger and Its Measurement Boundaries. Recorded and estimated ledgers must not be merged: iFlow's roughly 247 million tokens are estimated from character counts because its usage fields are zero, and stay outside every total here.

Author's thesis

Evidence must be snapshotted, never accumulated. A number without a cutoff time is not a measurement — it is a rumour with decimal places.

Volume I · Business & Requirements

Intent never starts with "AI"

Every product in this ecosystem began as a sentence in business language — and the sentence still stands with the word "AI" removed.

02 · Intent

Four business intents, none of them mentions AI

AI was brought in later as a means of execution. It was never the purpose.

MRRC · 2026-03-06 · ba66892

Hams needed a universal remote station interface — drive any Hamlib-capable radio from a browser, without rewriting the control logic for each radio. Save effort.

SunMRRC / SunsdrMobile · 2026-06-21 · 38cb85e

SunSDR2 DX users needed to bypass the desktop software and get synchronized control, spectrum, audio, and deterministic transmit safety. Safety.

MRRC FT-710 · 2026-07-06 · 9403e2e

The FT-710 already had a USB cable plugged into it — use that cable instead of the SCU-LAN10 accessory for remote operation. (Proven later: the in-cable FT4222 bridge yields ~21 fps of real spectrum, 48 kHz bidirectional Opus audio, and full CAT control.) Save money.

MRRC-FT8 · 2026-08-03

FT8 is not a single CAT command — it is a complete workflow of 15-second cycle synchronization, decode selection, a QSO state machine, and controlled transmit timing. Save time.

Author's thesis

First rule: if removing the word "AI" kills your business need, it was never a business need — it was a technology demo.

03 · Control group

Six months with nowhere for knowledge to land

The models were not weak. What was weak: knowledge had no carrier.

From 2025-10-20 to 2026-04-16, the same person ran 185 iFlow sessions and 37,202 turns across six model backends (qwen3-coder-plus, glm-5 — 10,420 turns in a single week — kimi-k2.5, minimax-m2.5). The output was real: MRRC's ancestor was born in this period. But nothing about the work settled anywhere.

Fact

Every one of the 37,202 assistant turns records zero usage, and no engineering artifact survived the era: no contract, no document system, no gate. When a session ended, what it had learned ended with it.

That is the problem every mechanism in Volume III exists to solve.

Volume takeaway

Intent exists in business language or it does not exist; and knowledge needs a carrier that outlives the conversation. Every mechanism in the volumes that follow exists for the second half.

Volume II · Process

From round-trips to right-first-time

"It got smoother" is a feeling; quantified, it is evidence. Three metrics, all reproducible from git history and local logs.

06 · Rework

Not a monotonic decline — an arc

Rework concentrates in exploration and integration weeks; maintenance weeks sit in single digits.

Fact

ft710's fix-commit share fell from 36% in its opening week to 6–12% in maintenance weeks; ft8's fell from 29% to 17% — achieved during its two highest-volume weeks (147 / 82 commits).

The counter-evidence is just as real, and it arrived this week. MRRC hit 50% fix share in its big integration week (W32, 798M tokens); modern hit 31% in its big refactor week (W35); sunsdr's early hardware-tuning weeks hit 51%. Last week mrrc_modern closed 41 commits of which 17 were fixes — a 41% fix share, its highest since the refactor. New requirements always bring new rework. The difference is who does the rework: humans intervened only 0.73 turns per commit, so the agent completed nearly all of it autonomously.

07 · Cadence

The workflow takes shape

Contract red first, agent delivers to green.

Fact

Release cadence kept accelerating: this week mrrc_modern shipped v1.14.0 through v1.14.3 — four releases in five days, with the version's own changelog corrected the same day it was published. Earlier: modern shipped 6 versions in 13 days (3 in a single day); ft8 shipped 3 versions in its first 3 days; MRRC once shipped 9 versions in two days.

Fact

Since 2026-W35 the website repo shows a stable "contract test goes red first, feature commit turns it green" pattern: 5 test→feat pairs, with a median time-to-green of 19 minutes. This article's own series is being built the same way.

Inference

Write the contract first and let AI deliver to green — an institutionalized capability that only formed late, and the organizational shape of "smooth".

Volume takeaway

Three curves fall in the same direction; rework follows an arc and concentrates in exploration weeks. The late-timeline difference is not zero rework — it is who does it: the agent, autonomously.

09 · Incident chains

The hardest evidence that the method works

Not one of the 52 constraints was designed cleverly in advance; every one traces back to a concrete incident.

FT-710 frequency drift · 13-day loop

DN; is not a DNR query but a VFO step command — polling it every 2 seconds drifted a live radio's frequency by ~20 Hz per call. Field incident (fixed 07-07, 72fd6f0) → design ruling AD-014 written into the SDD (07-08, bb128ff) → machine-readable constraint cat-no-dn (07-20, 2498ec2) → pre-edit interception, reproducible (exit code 2).

Deploy backups fill the disk · 7-day loop

08-29: server /var/tmp at 100% (about 300 MB per backup) → 09-05: sister fixes land in four repositories on the same day — and, following the thread, the hidden defect that portal backups had never worked (an unquoted remote heredoc, 067f565) → the rules are promoted to the four deployment iron rules.

Author's thesis

The incident conversion rate — the time from incident to permanent immunity — is the true measure of an organization's learning ability. This ecosystem runs at 13 days and 7 days. A registry that stopped growing is not automatically good news: it is a question, and the answer is in whether new incident rows appear when the next incident does.

10 · Delivery evidence

Two ledgers that never vouch for each other

Test suites were run, not read, on census day 2026-09-12.

Fact

FT-710: 439 passed. mrrc_modern: 724 passed, up from 682 a week earlier. ft8: 935 passed, 1 skipped, 1 xfailed — previously published as "937 collected", and collected is not passed; the wording had to change with the evidence. Note also that all three product repositories moved to their own virtualenvs (Python 3.13): running the suites under the system Python 3.9 now fails at collection time on X | None annotations. That is an environment limit, not a product defect, and the boundary is written down rather than quietly dropped.

Ledger A · Product maturity

  1. design target
  2. simulation
  3. automated test
  4. bench verified
  5. field verified
  6. released / operational
  7. known issue (the honest rung)

Ledger B · Process evidence

  1. registry entry exists
  2. spec / plan trail
  3. enforced inside sessions
  4. interception reproduced

The two ledgers never intersect: a pretty process ledger never vouches for a product claim. The open boundaries stay open in public: FT710Mobile has an unresolved P0 PTT safety issue, and the EFHW tuner's claim status stays at design-target until PCB fabrication and bench verification.

Author's thesis

You can delegate the doing; you can never delegate the "does it count". Responsibility cannot be delegated.

Volume takeaway

The method was not designed in advance; it grew out of incidents. Verdict → machine-readable constraint → pre-write interception is the loop — and all 52 constraints trace back to a specific decision or accident, not to a planning session.

Volume IV · Models

The model is not the long-term variable

Seven months, six eras, and one week that made the whole question smaller. The almanac lives in its own volume.

11 · Shift

The main force changed inside a week

Measured from local logs, not vendor announcements.

On 2026-09-05 this ecosystem's claude-code workload was dominated by one value-tier model. Seven days later the same harness is dominated by a different one: qwen3.8-max-0902 at 3,883 calls, with the previous leader still present but no longer driving. On the pi side, a model that did not appear in the last census at all — glm-5.3-flash — is already at 745 calls, and a vision variant has appeared in the logs.

Inference

Inside the previous "flash hegemony" there was constant challenger rotation, and the rotation did not stop — it accelerated. The model market turns over every three to four weeks. Any method that hard-codes a model name is buying a depreciating asset; the selection mechanism, not the choice, is the durable part.

The measured division of labour still holds — flagships buy direction, value models buy execution, long-context variants buy the specials, harnesses buy the way of working — and the full era-by-era record, the task × model × harness matching table, the public benchmarks (independent measurements and vendor claims kept in separate layers) and the routing-label trap are all in the companion volume The Model × Harness Almanac.

Volume takeaway

The main force of a harness can change in seven days. Do not lock in a model; lock in the way you choose one — and keep your own log, because it is the only benchmark that measures your work.

Volume V · Attribution & Advice

Where the efficiency actually came from

Two variables moved at once for seven months. Here is the evidence that separates them.

12 · Attribution

Stronger models, or a thicker method?

Both variables changed over the seven months; four pieces of evidence separate them.

1 · Same model, ±method: an order of magnitude

Flash exploring ft8 in W32 cost 75.5 M per commit; the same model in maintenance cost 0.9 M per commit. The model did not change — the contract thickness did.

2 · Strong models, no method: still zero

iFlow-era glm-5 produced 10,420 turns in a single week, yet the 37,202 turns left zero sediment. With method absent, model strength is beside the point.

3 · The method's birthday is precisely visible

The contract package grew in ft710 on 07-20 and was inherited wholesale by ft8 on 08-02 — and ft8's intervention turns fell from 17.1 straight to 0.3.

4 · Model upgrades widen the delegation radius

The task granularity one turn can reliably deliver grew from "change this function" to "deliver this feature against this spec and pass the contract tests". Models decide what can be delegated; method decides whether what was delegated comes back.

Conclusion · Inference

The attribution ratio cannot be separated precisely from this data, but the direction is clear: model capability sets the ceiling, and engineering method decides how much of that ceiling you actually capture. Of the two variables, only one is under your control.

13 · Advice

Two tracks

The engineering track is yours; the model track is the market's. The compound interest is on yours.

The engineering-capability track (you control it — the compound interest is here):

  1. Snapshot before you quote

    Any figure you intend to act on gets a cutoff timestamp and a written window. This article's ledger is stamped 2026-09-12T02:40:59Z, and every harness carries its own window, because one of them deletes its own history.

  2. Acceptance before implementation

    Write the acceptance conditions before the first line of code — the standard of "does it count" precedes implementation and is the number-one killer of round-trips.

  3. Machine-readable constraints, pre-write gates

    The fix is only the first link; ruling → machine-readable constraint → gate is the closed loop (this ecosystem: 13 days / 7 days).

  4. Red first, then green

    Contract tests first, agents deliver to green — the median loop in this ecosystem is 19 minutes, and the registry's plateau is watched rather than assumed.

The model-capability track (the market gives it — the strategy is not locking in):

  1. Pick tiers by task, not by faith

    Flagships for greenfield and blitz work, the value tier for daily work, long-context variants for specials — in measurement, no model wins every task, and this week the main force changed.

  2. Re-evaluate the market every 3–4 weeks

    The timeline above changed main force six times; the selection mechanism — fast trials plus a weekly review — matters more than any single choice.

  3. Beware model names

    Routing labels are not model entities, and vendor claims are not independent benchmarks. Your own logs — which task, how many tokens, how many round-trips, what result — are the only benchmark that never lies to you.

The checklists, templates and the case-derived failure library that turn these seven rules into something you can copy on Monday are in the companion volume.

14 · Closing

Ask where it settles first

Seven months of lessons fold into one sentence.

Author's thesis

First ask "where does my judgment settle", then ask "what can AI do for me".

AI use without a settling ground is consumption — 37,202 turns of conversation can leave nothing behind. AI use with a settling ground is asset accumulation — every incident, every constraint, every spec lowers the cost of the next delegation. And the ledger is the same: a number you snapshot and date is an asset; a number you remember and quote is weather. Models change generation every quarter; the contract thickens every day. The former is weather; the latter is climate. What you can build is the climate.

Volume takeaway

Models set the ceiling; method decides how much of it you collect. And the measurement itself obeys the same rule — snapshot it, date it, or it will quietly change under you.

Volume VI · The Four Dimensions

Separate judgment from execution

The distillation layer. Four dimensions explain everything the ledger showed — and they travel to any business, radio or not.

15 · The four dimensions

Four dimensions, four disciplines

The distillation: what the case means once the ledger is closed.

The one-sentence method

Agentic engineering is not "using AI to write code". It separates judgment from execution: judgment settles into contracts, execution is delegated to agents, and evidence decides whether a contract survives. That one sentence explains why the same operator left evaporated chat logs in the control period and seven repositories with 52 machine-readable constraints in the measured one — the difference was never the model; it was whether judgment had a carrier that outlives the conversation.

1 · Engineering history — the abstraction ladder rises again

Machine code → assembly → high-level languages → frameworks → cloud: every generation delegates one more layer of "how" to the machine. Agentic delegates the whole implementation layer. What remains for the person is three acts: define the intent, mark the boundaries, exercise the verdict. The workload moved accordingly — 12 design specs and 10 implementation plans were written before the code; ft8's first substantive commit brought AGENTS.md, a 15-chapter SDD and contract tests before any business code. Definition now precedes implementation.

2 · Epistemology — the carrier decides the knowledge's lifespan

Chat logs evaporate with the session; documents expire and get skipped; machine-readable constraints with runtime gates do not need to be remembered — they appear when needed. 52 constraints are 52 judgments made permanent, and they survived six harnesses because the carrier is the repository, not a tool. Incident-to-immunity time is the true measure of organizational learning: this ecosystem ran 13 days and 7 days.

3 · Economics — tokens are the price of uncertainty, and cache reads are the rent

Token spend follows uncertainty density, not line count, and the peaks land exactly where direction is undecided. In the three harnesses that report a complete split, cache reads account for 93.7% of all recorded tokens — the standing cost of re-sending context that has not been made machine-sliceable. Spending less is not "use AI less"; it is reducing the uncertainty itself, and shrinking the context that must be re-read.

4 · Responsibility — two ledgers, three non-delegable acts

Product maturity and process evidence are separate books that never vouch for each other: on census day, 439 passing FT-710 tests could not close one open P0 PTT safety issue in the client. Three acts are never delegable — hardware bring-up and bench debugging, RF safety on real radios, and the verdict on whether evidence is sufficient. Not because AI is too weak, but because responsibility cannot be delegated.

The four disciplines

1 · Definition before implementation — acceptance criteria, non-goals and boundaries written before the first line of code; intent phrased in business language that stands without the word AI. 2 · Every incident becomes a rule — repair is only the first link; verdict → constraint → pre-write interception closes the loop. 3 · Watch where uncertainty spends — token peaks are a direction radar; audit every important number against two sources, and date the snapshot. 4 · Keep the two ledgers apart — process badges never promote product claims; "do" can be delegated, "did it count" never leaves your hands.
